# The first 100 days after buying a digital business

*By Joash Boyton, CEO of [Acquiry](https://www.acquiry.com/), Melbourne*

Completion feels like the finish line. For the new owner it is the start. The first three months decide whether the business you bought keeps the customers, staff and momentum you paid for. This note sets out a simple plan I suggest buyers follow: stabilise, learn, then improve.

## Days 1 to 30: stabilise

- **Take control of every account.** Domains, hosting, code repositories, payment processors, app stores, ad accounts, email and analytics. Change credentials and remove old access in a planned order so nothing breaks.
- **Keep the lights on.** Do not change pricing, product or suppliers yet. Customers and staff are watching for signs of disruption.
- **Talk to the people.** Meet every team member and key contractor one to one. Say what will stay the same and when you will share plans.
- **Make the introductions.** Use the seller's transition support to meet the largest customers, suppliers and partners while goodwill is high.

## Days 31 to 60: learn

- **Rebuild the numbers yourself.** Set up monthly reporting from the source systems: revenue, churn, margin, cash. Compare it with what you saw in diligence.
- **Find the single points of failure.** People, platforms, suppliers or processes that the business could not run without.
- **List the quick wins and the slow fixes.** Separate small changes that are safe to make now from bigger changes that need more evidence.

## Days 61 to 100: improve

- **Pick two or three priorities.** Tie each to a number you track monthly.
- **Fix the risks you found.** Document key processes, add backup suppliers, reduce dependence on one channel.
- **Share a plan.** Tell the team, and where relevant customers, what will change over the next year and why.

## What to avoid

- Changing pricing, brand or core product in the first month
- Cutting costs before you understand what they pay for
- Assuming the seller's way of reporting matches yours

## A simple test

At day 100, can you produce the monthly numbers yourself, name every critical account holder, and explain your three priorities in one minute? If so, the transition has worked.
